An army on a front line spreads itself out and shifts as the border moves. With an offensive set and executing, it attacks on its own where the odds suit its stance, massing at the head of the arrow. A division you order by hand obeys you first, then returns to its army.

Sieges: fortresses must be besieged after their garrison is beaten. Divisions standing in the province add progress each day (artillery and sappers count for more). Add a Siege train support company in the designer to double a division’s weight in a siege, lift the daily limit and soften the penalty for attacking forts. The ring on the map shows progress.

Narrow fronts: if you attack with more troops than both the defenders and the province's combat width, the spare divisions march round the flanks and lengthen your line (most in open country, less in hills and passes; cavalry and good generals help). A defender who cannot fill the line is outflanked for up to +15% attack.

Give each army a general (click "No general" in the army bar). Shift-click several armies to form an army group under a field marshal. Divisions waiting on a front build up a planning bonus of up to +30% attack, used up as they attack.

Armies eat. Supply flows out from your capital, depots, fortresses, big towns and ports, thinning with distance; roads carry it further, rivers and straits hinder it. Too many divisions in poor country go hungry, and troops cut off from every source are encircled and will be destroyed if they break. Build a supply depot near the front (Industry, on a province) and check the Supply map mode.

Construction works as in HoI4: your builders come from civilian industry (less a share kept on consumer goods, set by the economy law). On the Construction tab, choose a building, then click your provinces on the map to queue it; projects are worked in queue order with up to 15 builders each, and roads speed the work. Mills, arsenals, dockyards and universities need a free building slot in their region. Research (T) is a tech tree by year: click to start research in a free slot or queue it; techs add slots, universities speed research, and great victories give one-off research bonuses.

Diplomacy is on each nation's card: propose an alliance or invite them into your faction (they show what they think of it), guarantee their independence, send volunteers to a nation at war, or send muskets, cannon, uniforms, horses and gold. Occupied land resists: partisans strike at garrisons and an unguarded province may rise; keep troops in restless provinces (Resistance map mode). When a nation surrenders to you, the peace conference opens and the victors take turns spending points on regions, gold or a client state.

The sea is divided into regions (click the water). Click a fleet counter (ships on the sea, or anchored by a harbour) to select it, then right-click a sea to send it there, or one of your harbours to base it there. Missions: patrol finds and holds a sea and blockades enemy harbours on it; strike force fights whatever is found; raiders take enemy merchantmen; escorts guard yours. Damaged or tired fleets come home to repair. Shipping troops needs convoys and is dangerous where the enemy holds the sea. Build dockyards in harbours to lay down ships (Navy tab, N).

Snow, mud, rain and summer heat slow marches, weaken attacks and wear down men away from home. Russia in January is a bad idea. A nation surrenders once it loses 65% of its victory points (45% if its capital has been held three months); see the Victory points map mode. With fog of war on, you only see enemies near your land and troops; cavalry and scouts see further.
